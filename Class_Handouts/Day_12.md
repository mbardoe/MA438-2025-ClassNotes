# Lesson: Review – Logarithms and Exponential Equations

## Part I: Be Careful with Domains!

Sometimes, logarithmic equations can have **extraneous solutions**—values that seem to work algebraically but make a logarithm undefined.

### Example:
Solve: $$ \log_6(x - 2) + \log_6(x + 3) = 1 $$

Step 1: Combine the logs using the product rule:
$$ \log_6((x - 2)(x + 3)) = 1 $$

Step 2: Do "6 to the" to both sides canceling the logarithm:
$$ (x - 2)(x + 3) = 6^1 = 6 $$

Step 3: Expand and solve:
$$x^2 + x - 6 = 6 \Rightarrow x^2 + x - 12 = 0 \Rightarrow (x + 4)(x - 3) = 0 \Rightarrow x = -4 \text{ or } x = 3 $$

Step 4: Check both:
- If $x = -4$: $\log_6(-4 - 2)$ is undefined 
- If $x = 3$: both logs are defined 

### Final Answer: \( x = 3 \)

## Part III: Application Problems – Solve Using Logarithms

9. A population of bacteria doubles every 3 hours. If the initial population is 200, how long until the population reaches 3200?

\vspace{3cm}

10. A sample of a radioactive substance decays by 5% each hour. How many hours will it take for a 100 g sample to decay to 40 g?

\vspace{3cm}

11. A savings account offers 6% interest compounded annually. How long will it take for $\$1,000$ to grow to $\$1,800$?

\vspace{3cm}

12. How long will it take for $\$500$ to triple at 4.5% interest compounded continuously?

\vspace{3cm}

13. The population of a town is modeled by $P(t) = 12000 \cdot 1.025^t$. How long will it take for the population to reach 15,000?

\vspace{3cm}

14. The mass of a radioactive substance is modeled by $M(t) = 80 \cdot (0.94)^t$. After how many days will the mass be 50 g?

\vspace{3cm}

15. A virus spreads so that the number of cases triples every 6 days. If there are 150 cases today, how long will it take to exceed 5,000 cases?

\vspace{3cm}

16. You invest $\$2,000$ at 5% interest compounded quarterly. How long until the investment reaches $\$3,000$?
