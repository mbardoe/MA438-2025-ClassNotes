## Part II: Motivation for Logarithms

Sometimes we can't solve an exponential equation using the algebra of plus, minus, times, divide, and root alone. 
For example:

- $2^x = 17$
- $5^{2x - 1} = 100$

In these cases, we need a new tool. That tool is called a **logarithm**.

### What is a Logarithm?

A logarithm is the **inverse of an exponential**. It tells us what exponent is needed to reach a value. For example:

- $2^3 = 8$ means that $\log_2(8) = 3$
- $3^4 = 81$ means that $\log_3(81) = 4$

More generally:

$$
\text{If } b^x = y, \text{ then } \log_b(y) = x
$$

- $b$ is the **base**
- $y$ is the **value**
- $x$ is the **exponent**

### Analogies

- To undo squaring, we take a square root: if $x^2 = 25$, then $x = \sqrt{25} = 5$.
- To undo cubing, we take a cube root.
- To undo "2 to the power," we take $\log_2$
- To undo "3 to the power," we take $\log_3$

---

## Logarithms vs. Division

It's important not to confuse logarithms with division. They answer very different types of questions:

- A division statement like $12 \div 4 = 3$ means you would **add** 4 together 3 times to get 12.
- A logarithm like $\log_2(8) = 3$ means you would **multiply** 2 together 3 times to get 8.

Division is about repeated addition, but logarithms are about repeated multiplication.

This is why logarithms are especially useful for reversing exponential growth — just like how division helps reverse multiplication.

---

## Logarithms of Numbers Less Than 1

When you evaluate a logarithm of a number **less than 1**, the result is **negative**.

Why? Because you're asking:  
"What power must I raise this base to in order to get a number smaller than 1?"

That means you're dealing with **division** or **negative exponents**.

### Examples

- $\log_2(1/8) = -3$ because $2^{-3} = 1/8$

Because you would need to divide 1 three times by 2 to get 8. Similiarly, $\log_2(8) = 3$ because $2^{3} = 8$ 
Because you must multiply 1 by 2 three times to get 8. All multiplications and division "starts" at 1. 

- $\log_{10}(0.01) = -2$ because $10^{-2} = 0.01$
- $\log_3(1/3) = -1$ because $3^{-1} = 1/3$

### Key Idea

If $b^x = y$ and $0 < y < 1$, then $x$ must be **negative**.
