## I. Introduction: Graphing the Exponential Function

### Basic Form:  
$$
f(x) = b^x
$$

Where:
- $b > 0$ and $b \ne 1$
- If $b > 1$: **increasing function**
- If $0 < b < 1$: **decreasing function**

---

### Key Characteristics of the Graph:
- Domain: all real numbers
- Range: $(0, \infty)$
- $f(0) = 1$
- Asymptote: $y = 0$
- Graph passes through: $(0, 1), (1, b), (-1, \frac{1}{b})$

## II. The Two Forms of Exponential Functions

### **1. Percentage Growth/Decay Form:**

$$
A(t) = A_0(1 + r)^t
$$

Where:
- $A_0$ is the initial value
- $r$ is the **rate of change** (written as a decimal)
- $t$ is time

This form is similar to the linear function:

$$
L(x) = mx + b
$$

| Linear | Exponential |
|--------|-------------|
| Starts at $b$ | Starts at $A_0$ |
| Adds $m$ per unit | Multiplies by $(1 + r)$ per unit |

---

### **2. Repeated Multiplication Form (Doubling/Half-life):**

$$
A(t) = A_0 \cdot b^{\frac{t}{d}}
$$

Where:
- $A_0$ is the initial value
- $b$ is the growth/decay **multiplier** (e.g., 2 for doubling, $\frac{1}{2}$ for half-life)
- $d$ is the number of time units it takes to multiply by $b$ once

> This form is useful when you know how often the value doubles, halves, triples, etc.

\vspace{3cm}
